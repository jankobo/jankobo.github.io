What you can do in Jankobo

Jankobo is a riichi mahjong study tool that opens right in your browser. It starts with a tutorial classroom for complete beginners and goes on to what-to-discard problems, matches under tournament rule presets, online rooms for playing with friends, and themes that change the tiles and the table. For each feature, this page says where to open it from the first screen and what you can do there. No install, no account, and it is free.

Open Jankobo

Learn

Mahjong Classroom: a riichi mahjong tutorial on a line map

Tap "Classroom" on the first screen and a lesson map shaped like a subway map opens. Each station is one lesson, and there are 20 lines and more than 90 stations across three tiers: Basics, Intermediate and Advanced. They start with the tiles and the table and go on to riichi, calling tiles, scoring, shanten and tenpai, yaku and yakuman reference lines, dice and the wall, table manners, and tournament rules with their uma and oka scores.

Most lessons run on the real match screen. The coach tells you in one line what to do now, and you move on by pressing that tile or button yourself. Topics that are quicker to see as a picture, such as the fu table or the score table, open as a lecture screen instead.

Stations never lock, so you can open any of them directly. After your very first hand you can also pick "Straight to a match" to skip the classroom and go play. The whole path from the first launch to reviewing your games has its own page.

Nanikiru practice: 800 what-to-discard problems

Open "Practice" on the first screen and choose "What would you discard?" to get positions where it is your turn to discard and you pick one tile. There are 800 problems split into four tiers: Intro, Basic, Advanced and Expert. Most of them were cut from matches that four bots played, and spots with and without a riichi are mixed together.

The answer is the tile chosen by Mortal, an openly published mahjong AI. Mortal's choice scores 100 and Mortal's second-ranked pick scores 50, and the tiers are set by how clear Mortal's choice was. Only your first answer counts. The answers were computed in advance; Mortal itself is not part of the app.

Answering opens the explanation. Its "Choices by engine" panel lines up Mortal, Mortal's second pick, kobalab, Jankobo's basic recommender and your choice, and compares the distance to tenpai, the kinds and number of tiles that improve the hand, and the yaku you could aim for. If someone has declared riichi, safety is shown as well.

You can also make problems from your own games. "Mine problems from replays" pulls problems from your three most recent replays, and the screen says that these are graded by kobalab's engine, not by Mortal.

Play

A 3D table that moves like an offline mahjong table

The first hand of a match starts the way it would at a real table. The four wind tiles are drawn to pick a temporary dealer, and two rolls of the dice decide the first dealer. Then the wall is broken at the count of the dice and the hands are dealt four tiles at a time from the break. Classroom lessons skip this and online matches shorten it. Under "Round start" in the settings you can choose "First round", "Every round" or "Off".

Declaring riichi throws a riichi stick onto the table. When someone wins, the winning tile goes down first, a burst of light follows, then the rest of the hand is laid down, and the point sticks fly over one by one and stack beside the receiver's wall. If change is due, 100-point or 1,000-point sticks come back. The scoreboard in the middle lies flat on the felt, turns each player's score toward that player, and also shows the round, the tiles left, honba and deposited riichi sticks.

During a match, the view follows the shape of your screen. A wide screen (a desktop window or a phone turned sideways) shows a 3D table with perspective, and a phone held upright shows a 2D table seen from straight above. Turning the phone during a match switches on the spot; there is no separate setting. Replays, the mistake notebook and nanikiru always use the 2D table seen from above, whatever the shape of the screen.

Practice JPML-A and KML tournament rules

In "Sandbox", under "Make" on the first screen, you set the rules yourself and start a match. Pick one of three presets, "Standard", "JPML-A" or "KML", then change single rows under "Rule options".

Each preset switches different things. JPML-A plays without ippatsu, ura dora, kan dora or red fives, and without kazoe yakuman. KML uses kiriage mangan, has no nagashi mangan, no bust and no abortive draws, and counts a double-wind pair as 2 fu. "Standard" is Jankobo's default: three red fives with ippatsu, ura dora and kan dora all on.

The "Rule options" rows include kuitan, ippatsu, ura and kan dora, the number of red fives, kazoe and double yakuman, kiriage mangan, daisangen, daisuushi and suukantsu liability, nagashi mangan, "Multiple ron", abortive draws, bust, agari-yame and "Extra rounds". "Multiple ron" is one of "Atama-hane", "Double ron" or "Triple ron", and with "Extra rounds" on, a final hand where the leader is below the target continues as sudden death for up to one more wind. The target is 30,000 in Sandbox and the room's "Points needed for 1st" in a friend room.

The final tournament tally, the score with uma and oka, is set under "Rule options" too. Set "Uma" to 5-10, 10-20, 10-30 or "JPML float uma" and the "Return point" and "Ties" rows open up; when the match ends, every place on the result screen carries a score, and the breakdown splits it per player into points, uma and oka. "KML" turns on 10-20 uma with a 30,000 return point, and "JPML-A" turns on "JPML float uma" with tied places sharing. The presets leave the starting points alone, so to start from 30,000 as the league's official rules do, change the starting points yourself.

What the three presets change and how the score is worked out are taught in the classroom's Advanced tier, on "Line 19 Event rules & score". It works the same hand out under "Standard" and under a preset side by side, then gives score practice problems and shows where in this game you pick "Uma".

There are always four seats, and you choose the mix of people and NPCs from "Me 1 + NPC 3" to "All me"; with two or more people, everyone takes turns on one screen. Each NPC seat gets its own strength on a nine-step scale from Novice to Boss, and you can set the starting hands and the wall yourself to build the position you want to drill. Up to five setups can be saved and loaded again.

Friend rooms use the same rules. When you create a room, "Detailed rules" offers the same three presets and every rule option above.

Online friend rooms: play riichi mahjong with friends online

On the first screen, open "Online friend room" and press "Create a room" to get a six-character room code. Friends join by typing that code under "Join a room", or by opening the link you send with "Copy invite link". There is no account and no login; you only pick a nickname for the room.

A room has four seats. If fewer than four people show up, the host can fill up to two empty seats with "Add NPC", so two or three friends can still play a full match. The host picks each NPC's strength.

The host chooses the format (East-only or hanchan), the starting points, "Points needed for 1st", "Turn time (s)" and the rules under "Detailed rules". With the 20+5 turn time, every decision gets 5 seconds, and anything longer comes out of a 20-second bank that refills each hand. 60, 300 and 600 seconds are also available.

Room setups can be kept with "Save / load" (up to five, or moved as a file), and during a match the ☰ menu's "Room info" shows the room code and rules again. Turning on "Shared look" puts the host's custom theme and characters on everyone's screen.

In a room with uma on, everyone's result screen shows the same scores and breakdown when the match ends. After a game, back in the lobby, a "Room record" button appears: one table with every game played in this room, each player's place and points, and for uma games the score total. When the players have changed and the host presses ready, the host is asked whether to reset the room record or keep it.

What reaches the server during online play, and what is not kept, is on a separate page.

Customize

Custom theme: your own mahjong tiles and table

Open "Custom theme" on the first screen to build a theme that changes the tiles, the table and the screen colors. For tiles you can set the front, back and edge colors, replace any of the 37 tile faces and the back with an image of your choice, or change only the ink color tile by tile. The table takes an image as well as a color, and animated images work too. Red fives, the dora highlight, point sticks, the screen background, buttons, text color and brightness each have their own setting.

A theme can carry up to six "My characters". Give each a name, portrait, play style, strength and lines for different situations, and they can sit at the table as opponents in Sandbox. Your own portrait for the table is part of the theme too.

You can keep three themes and switch between them with "Apply". "Export" saves a theme as a single JSON file, so a friend can load it with "Import" and apply it as is. Creating and editing work only in a desktop browser; on a phone you can import a theme and apply it.

Separately from themes, "My Profile" sets your name and portrait. In friend rooms, the people in the same room see that name and portrait.

In fairness

Online play is friend rooms only, where people who know each other meet with a room code. There is no matchmaking with strangers, no ranked play, no chat and no spectating; during a match you can only send six fixed emotes.

Sandbox and friend rooms are four-player only. There are no three-player matches.

Scores with uma and oka appear only on the result screen of Sandbox and friend-room matches with uma on, and in a friend room's room record. The score diagrams in the classroom only show how the sum is done with example numbers; classroom matches carry no score. The in-match scoreboard and replays carry no score, and NPCs play as usual without regard to it. Some values were set without checking the original rule text, such as the float uma when nobody or everybody finishes above the return point. A friend room's "Room record" lives only in each player's browser tab and is gone once you leave the room or close the tab.

The answers to the 800 nanikiru problems are the choice of a single engine, Mortal, and those positions come from bot matches, not human games. Problems mined from your replays are graded by kobalab's engine instead. That makes the set a different kind of thing from problems built on professional game records.
